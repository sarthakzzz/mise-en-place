A mastering chain for beginners

Mastering sounds scary, like the final exam of music. It doesn't have to be. Here is a simple chain that finishes a track without overcooking it, plus the one rule that matters most: compare at the same loudness.

By The Chef, Plugins for Breakfast · 13 November 2026

1Season gently

Using Spice Shelf EQ, our EQ plugin

Mastering EQ moves are small. If you need big boosts, go back to the mix.

With Spice Shelf EQ, lift or lower each jar a touch: Hearty for weight, Zesty or Crispy for air. Use the mortar and pestle (Tame) to soften harsh peaks. Its presets are level-matched, so you hear tone, not volume.

2Glue it together

Using Popcorn Sandwich Compressor, our compressor plugin

A little compression makes a mix feel like one piece instead of separate parts.

Turn the Popcorn Sandwich dial only slightly left and choose Smooth Opto or Warm Vari-Mu for gentle glue. Use the sieve so the kick doesn't drive all the compression.

3Shave the peaks

Using Ghost Pepper Clipper, our clipper plugin

A clipper before the final limiter catches short peaks, so the track can get louder with less pumping.

Pick the soft bell pepper shape in Ghost Pepper Clipper and keep the drive modest. Check with the spoon (Taste) that you are only clipping a thin edge.

4Keep the low end in mono

Using Butter Spread, our free stereo widener plugin

Bass in stereo can cancel out on club systems, on phones and when a song is played in mono.

Our free Butter Spread pulls the bass into mono with the toaster lever and dial (anywhere from 40 to 400 Hz) while you keep the width up top. Try somewhere around 100 to 150 Hz as a starting point.

5Finish and hit your loudness target

Using Cherry on the Top Mastering, our mastering plugin

Streaming services turn loud songs down, so chasing extreme loudness only costs you punch.

In Cherry on the Top, play the loudest part once so the kitchen scale learns your level, pick a ticket (Apple, Spotify, Loud or Club), choose a style, then turn the cake dial. The bell sets a true-peak ceiling and the spoon compares at matched level, so you hear honestly whether it got better or just louder.

Questions from the dining room

How loud should I master for Spotify?

Spotify normalises playback to about -14 LUFS by default, and Apple Music to about -16 LUFS. Mastering much louder than that mostly costs dynamics.

What order should a mastering chain be in?

A common order is EQ, compression, saturation or clipping, then a limiter last. Fix tone first, control dynamics next, set the final level at the end.

Can I master inside my mix session?

Yes, on the master bus. Just leave a little headroom in the mix and compare before and after at the same loudness.
