Creative delay tricks for producers

A delay is the most fun you can have with a time machine. Here are six tricks that go beyond a polite echo, from classic vocal throws to grains scattered over a breakfast bowl.

By The Chef, Plugins for Breakfast · 13 November 2026

1Vocal throws that stay out of the way

Using Spaghetti Delay, our delay plugin

Automate a send so only the last word of a line echoes, then let the delay duck while the singer is singing.

In Spaghetti Delay, sync the crank to 1/4 or dotted 1/8, set a moderate fork twirl (feedback) and turn up the cloche (ducking). The repeats bloom only in the gaps.

2Ping-pong and freeze

Using Spaghetti Delay, our delay plugin

Bounce repeats between the speakers with the plates (ping-pong) for width, and hit the gelato (freeze) to capture the last repeats as a loop. It is a great way to make a transition from a single phrase.

3Glitchy repeats on random steps

Using Popping Candy, our glitch and stutter plugin

A stutter on the last beat of a bar is a delay trick too.

Popping Candy's repeat, reverse and tape stop jars pop on random steps in time with your track. Lower the chance on each jar for occasional surprises, or lock the lids on a pattern you like for a repeatable fill.

4Granular shimmer echoes

Using Muesli Granular, our granular plugin

Grains of the recent past, pitched and scattered, sound like a sparkling, smeared delay.

In Muesli Granular use the spoon (Stir) to read further back in time, add honey (Drizzle) for feedback and choose the +7 dish for upward shimmer. Pour the milk jug (Freeze) to turn a moment into a pad.

5Echoes that move

Using Hot-o-Pan, our auto-pan plugin

Put an auto-panner after the delay so each repeat lands in a different place.

Hot-o-Pan synced to the song with a square or random shape throws repeats left and right, and the honey jar keeps any bass centred.

Questions from the dining room

What is a delay throw?

An echo on just one word or phrase, usually done by automating the send to the delay, so the vocal stays clean the rest of the time.

What delay time should I use?

Tempo-synced 1/4, 1/8 and dotted 1/8 are the classics. Dotted 1/8 gives a bouncy, rolling feel.

Why does my delay make the mix muddy?

Too much feedback and too many low frequencies. Ducking, a high-pass filter on the delay, and lower feedback clean it up.
