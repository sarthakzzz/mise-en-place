How to get a lo-fi tape sound in any DAW

Lo-fi is the art of making things sound a little worse, beautifully. Think of it as toast: the best bits are slightly burnt. Here is how to toast a clean loop.

By The Chef, Plugins for Breakfast · 13 November 2026

1Put it on cassette

Using Burnt Toast Tape Machine, our tape saturation plugin

Tape is the heart of lo-fi: soft saturation, a gentle roll-off of the highs and a pitch that never quite sits still.

On the loop or the whole beat, pick cassette tape in Burnt Toast Tape Machine and the slowest speed (7.5 ips) for the softest top end. Add agar jelly and crumbs (wow and flutter) until the pitch wobbles like an old Walkman, then a little kettle (hiss). The honey (high cut) mellows anything still too shiny.

2Round off the edges

Using Noodle Filter, our filter plugin

Lo-fi lives in the middle. Taking off some lows and highs makes a loop sound like it is playing through an old speaker.

Use Noodle Filter in band-pass mode with low resonance, or low-pass to just darken things. A drop of chilli oil (drive) adds grit. Automate the chopsticks down for a 'playing in the next room' moment before the drop.

3Add a seasick shimmer

Using Milkshake Chorus, our chorus plugin

A slow, slightly detuned chorus makes keys and guitars feel like warped vinyl.

Choose the tape flavour in Milkshake Chorus, slow the mixer speed dial right down and keep one or two straws. Pour on the whipped cream (high cut) so the chorus stays soft.

4Echoes from an old machine

Using Spaghetti Delay, our delay plugin

Lo-fi delays are dark, a bit wobbly and never too loud.

Pick the lo-fi character on Spaghetti Delay's pantry shelf, sync it to a dotted 1/8 and add a little tagliatelle (wobble). Use the cloche (ducking) so the echoes only bloom in the gaps.

Questions from the dining room

What makes music sound lo-fi?

Mostly tape character (saturation, wow and flutter, hiss), a narrower frequency range, and slightly loose timing and pitch.

Should I put the tape effect on every track or the master?

Both work. On the master everything is glued with the same character; on single tracks you can keep drums cleaner and make keys dustier.

Does this work in GarageBand?

Yes. On a Mac the AU versions load in GarageBand and Logic.
