How to make a techno rumble

That rolling, growling low end under a warehouse kick isn't magic. It is a kick, a big reverb and a bit of heat, all kept on a leash. Here is how to bake it.

By The Chef, Plugins for Breakfast · 13 November 2026

1Make a big, dark tail from the kick

Using Bread Oven Reverb, our reverb plugin

Copy your kick to a new channel (or use a send) and put Bread Oven Reverb on it at 100% wet. Sourdough (hall) or the tin (chamber) give a long, dense tail.

Turn the brick oven dial up for a long decay and use the bloomer crust (high damping) to darken it. The treacle jar (bass decay) lets the lows hang on longer than the highs, which is exactly the rumble we want.

2Heat it up

Using Burnt Toast Tape Machine, our tape saturation plugin

A clean reverb tail is too polite. Drive it into tape and it thickens and growls.

Add Burnt Toast Tape Machine after the reverb and turn up the heat until the toast browns. Vintage tape adds weight; a touch of head bump (the bagel) fattens the low mids. Skip hiss here; this is the dark corner of the kitchen.

3Keep only the low end

Using Noodle Filter, our filter plugin

Rumble lives under roughly 200 Hz. Everything above it just clutters the mix.

Put Noodle Filter in low-pass mode and lower the chopsticks until only the low growl remains, with the 24 dB slope for a cleaner cut. For movement, give the lucky cat (LFO) a slow tempo-synced sweep, or use the rice cooker (envelope follower) so the filter breathes with the groove.

4Duck it under the kick

Using Gummy Gate, our gate plugin

The rumble has to get out of the way every time the kick hits, or the two fight and the low end turns to mud.

Put Gummy Gate in ducker mode on the rumble channel and key it from the kick. Each kick pushes the rumble down, and it swells back in between. That push and pull is the groove. Popcorn Sandwich with its sieve does a similar job if you prefer a compressor.

Questions from the dining room

Do I need a sidechain for a techno rumble?

Yes, some kind of ducking keyed from the kick keeps the rumble and kick from fighting. A ducker or a compressor with an external key both work.

Should the rumble be in mono?

Keep it mostly mono. Low frequencies in stereo can cancel on club systems and vinyl. Butter Spread's toaster lever keeps bass in mono below a frequency you choose.

Which reverb type works best?

Long, dense spaces like halls and chambers. Short rooms don't give enough tail to rumble.
