How to make build-ups and transitions

The drop only hits as hard as the build-up before it. Here is a menu of transition tricks, from a classic filter sweep to a full food fight.

By The Chef, Plugins for Breakfast · 13 November 2026

1The classic filter sweep

Using Noodle Filter, our filter plugin

Automate a high-pass filter rising over the last 8 or 16 bars to thin the track out, then let everything slam back in on the drop.

Use Noodle Filter's high-pass mode and lift the chopsticks over the build. Curl them sideways for a bit more resonance as it climbs, for that rising whistle.

2Stutters and tape stops

Using Popping Candy, our glitch and stutter plugin

Speeding up repeats and a tape stop right before the drop are crowd favourites.

Popping Candy can repeat at shorter and shorter steps (from 1/4 down to 1/64), and its tape stop jar does the record-slowing-down move. Lock a pattern you like so it lands the same every time.

3Freeze the last moment

Using Bread Oven Reverb, our reverb plugin

Hold the reverb tail of the last chord or vocal and let it hang over the silence before the drop.

Bread Oven Reverb's freezer holds the tail forever. Automate it on at the end of the build, then off at the drop.

4Play the transition live

Using Food Fight, our performance multi-effect plugin

Sometimes the best transitions are performed, not drawn.

Hold a food in Food Fight to throw its effect in time with your song: Freeze, Spin, Whoosh and Splat are made for this. Record the holds as automation, or let the catapult throw foods by itself for happy chaos.

Questions from the dining room

How long should a build-up be?

Usually 8 or 16 bars in dance music, but it depends on the genre. Shorter builds keep energy high; longer ones work for big festival drops.

Should I cut the bass before the drop?

Often, yes. Removing the low end (with a high-pass filter) during the build makes the drop feel bigger when it comes back.

Can I perform these effects live?

Yes. Food Fight and Popping Candy follow your DAW's tempo, so they work in live sets.
