How to make trap drums hit harder

Your 808 is booming, your hi-hats are rolling, but the drums still sound like they are whispering through a pillow. Here is our kitchen-tested recipe for trap drums that slap, in four courses.

By The Chef, Plugins for Breakfast · 13 November 2026

1Start with a clean plate: tighten the hits

Using Gummy Gate, our gate plugin

Punch starts with space. Long kick tails and room noise smear into the 808 and blur the transient, so trim them before you add anything.

Put Gummy Gate on the kick or snare. Drag the licorice lace (threshold) until only the hit gets through, keep the gummy bear (hold) short and let the worm (release) close just before the next hit. Use the sieve to filter the key so a loud 808 doesn't open the snare's gate.

2Add crunch so the drums cut on small speakers

Using Orange Crusher, our distortion and saturation plugin

Phones and laptop speakers can't play deep sub, so the ear listens for harmonics instead. A little saturation adds them, which makes the kick and 808 feel louder without moving the meter much.

On the drum bus, press Orange Crusher down gently and try the tape or tube pulp for warmth. Push the mango (Zest) for extra transient punch, and the peel spiral (Rind) if the low end needs more weight. Keep the glass (mix) under 100% if you want the clean hit underneath.

3Compress for snap, not for squash

Using Popcorn Sandwich Compressor, our compressor plugin

A compressor with a medium attack lets the first crack of the snare through and then pulls the tail down, which reads as snap.

Turn the Popcorn Sandwich dial a little to the left and pick Punch FET. If the drums start to sound flat, set the popcorn bucket (mix) to about 50% for instant parallel compression: the body of the squashed drums with the bite of the dry ones. Use the sieve so the 808 doesn't pump the whole bus.

4Clip for loudness, keep the knock

Using Ghost Pepper Clipper, our clipper plugin

Clipping shaves the very top of each peak, so you can turn the drums up without the pumping a limiter can cause.

Put Ghost Pepper Clipper last on the drum bus. Start with a soft bell or jalapeño shape and raise the drive until the drums get louder. Turn on the ice glass (bass saver) so the 808 stays round, and press the spoon (taste) to hear only what you are clipping: if it sounds like crackle rather than a thin edge, back off.

Questions from the dining room

Should I clip or limit trap drums?

Many producers do both: a clipper on the drum bus catches the sharpest peaks so the limiter on the master works less. Clipping keeps transients sharper; limiting is cleaner on long, sustained sounds.

Why do my drums sound loud in my headphones but weak on a phone?

Small speakers can't play deep sub, so a kick or 808 with no upper harmonics nearly disappears. Gentle saturation adds harmonics the phone can play.

Can I do this in FL Studio, Ableton or Logic?

Yes. These plugins come as VST3 for Windows and Mac, AU for Logic and GarageBand, and AAX for Pro Tools on Mac, so the same chain works in any of them.
